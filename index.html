Reasonable Doubt  /  an evidence check for academic misconduct decisions

A flag is not
a finding.

A detector says a submission is 98% AI-written. That number is P(flag | cheating). The question in front of you is P(cheating | flag). They are not the same number, and on the published figures they are not even close.

Seven steps, in order. The detector score stays hidden until step four, and that ordering is the point.

Why does the base rate matter more than the score?

A detector tells you how often it fires on work that did use AI. It cannot tell you how often the work in front of you used AI, because that also depends on how many people in your cohort do it at all.

If almost nobody cheats, even an accurate detector spends most of its alarms on innocent people, simply because there are so many more of them to get wrong.

HEPI/Kortext Student Generative AI Survey 2026, n ≈ 10,000 UK students. 12% report putting AI-generated text directly into assessed work, up from 8% in 2025 and 3% in 2024. That figure is self-reported, so treat it as a floor rather than a measurement.

Your working base rate is 12 in 100. Multipliers are illustrative, not measured. They are here so the base rate can be defended as a judgement rather than smuggled in as a fact.
Why am I being asked this before I see the score?

Because how suspicious a case looks depends on how common the behaviour is to begin with. The same flag means something different in a supervised exam than in an unsupervised essay written over three weeks.

These multipliers are illustrative rather than measured, and they are shown as numbers on purpose. A number you can see is one you can argue with. An unspoken hunch about "this kind of student" is not.

Set your standard of proof

How certain would you need to be before you open a formal misconduct case? Decide now, while you still do not know the answer.

Most UK universities decide misconduct on the balance of probabilities, which is anything above 50%. UCL puts it plainly in its Academic Manual, where adjudicators "should apply 'on the balance of probabilities' as the standard of proof".
Why does the threshold get locked?

Because a standard you set after seeing the answer is not a standard. Kunda showed that people reach the conclusion they want by quietly building a justification for it, without ever feeling dishonest. A bar you can still move is a bar that will move.

You can start a new case at any point. You just cannot lower this one to fit the evidence you are about to see.

Locked thresholds cannot be edited. Start a new case to change it.

What would actually clear your bar

A second detector is not a second opinion. Running another tool over the same text does not multiply in, because the two tools react to the same surface features, so their errors arrive together. Treating correlated evidence as independent is the fastest way to manufacture certainty you have not earned. Every item above is listed because it comes from a different source than the text itself.

This tool estimates one quantity only, which is the probability that a flagged submission contains undeclared AI text, given a base rate and a stated detector accuracy. It is a reasoning aid for the person deciding whether to open a case. It is not a determination of misconduct, and it does not replace an institution's procedure or a student's right to respond.
